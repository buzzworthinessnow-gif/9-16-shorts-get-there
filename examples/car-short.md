# Example: How Did We Get Here? The Car — Short

**Status:** production example, not a finished or fully fact-checked video. Target **~53 seconds, 9:16**.

**Angle:** The car was not an instant gasoline-powered invention; different power sources competed before one approach became widespread. Avoid claiming a single universal inventor.

## Sample narration (~115 words, adjust to actual voice)

“Your car can warn you before a crash. But the first challenge? Making a carriage move without a horse. In the 1700s, experimenters built heavy steam-powered road machines. Later, inventors tried electricity and gasoline, too. In the 1880s, Karl Benz built a purpose-designed gasoline automobile. But a working machine wasn't enough. Bertha Benz's famous road journey helped show what practical driving demanded. Then factories found ways to build cars faster and cheaper. Roads filled up. Safety, pollution, and fuel use became new problems. Today, electric motors and computers are changing cars again. So the car wasn't one magical invention. It was a long chain of fixes. Subscribe for more, and comment: what should we rewind next?”

**Fact check before final narration:** confirm exact milestones and nuance around Cugnot, the Benz automobile, Bertha's journey, and mass production. Do not use the disputed “first car crash” anecdote as fact.
